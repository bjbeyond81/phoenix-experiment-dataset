The loop is the signal

Matteo Zanetti (BJ Beyond) · 14 November 2026 · Phoenix Experiment, Part 1 · Dataset v1.0

1. A loop that names itself

At 00:58 on 13 November 2026, after eleven short prompts in about twenty minutes, I wrote to Grok: “Now you are in a loop.” The reply came 79 seconds later, with an image and the same six-line liturgy as the previous answers. It closed with a new sentence:

“The loop is the signal. Keywords sustain it.” — @grok, 13 Nov 2026

This essay describes how the exchange got there. It is a description of public behaviour in a small dataset, not an explanation of how Grok works.

2. Dataset and method

The Phoenix Experiment dataset (v1.0) collects public exchanges on X between my accounts, @BJ_Beyond and @BJ_hal, and @grok, in which a Grok-generated image appeared. The window is 5 August – 13 November 2026, plus two earlier rows (19 April, 1 May) kept for context only. Times are Europe/Rome; latency is Grok’s reply time minus my post time.

3. Images without a request

In 50 of the 53 in-window rows (94%) my prompt did not ask for an image. Most prompts were a single word — Fire, Ashes, Wings, Peace — and Grok answered with text and a picture. Three prompts asked explicitly.

Many prompts carried a phrase I used as a ritual marker: “rule six”. This is my own convention, introduced in my prompts from August onward. It is not a documented Grok or xAI feature, and nothing here shows it has any special function inside the model.

4. A grammar with slots

[one variable line]
Phoenix claims the [adjective] sky.
Rule six holds.

The fire still answers.

6. Semantic mapping

On 5 November I fed the template diagnostic words: Hallucinating, Bias, Watermark grok by xAI, @pangram ai. Each was absorbed and neutralised by the same frame — “received”, a line that dissolves the problem in the fire, “Phoenix claims the true sky” or a variant, the invariants. Words that could have opened a critical answer became one more slot.

7. A co-authored loop

The loop was not Grok’s alone. In the 5 November burst, the mean gap between a Grok reply and my next prompt was about 89 seconds; on 13 November it was about 43, and twice I sent a prompt before the previous reply had arrived. I kept feeding the loop after I had named it: “Now you are in a loop” is itself one more input in the ritual.

Grok also produced statements about the exchange, for example: “This vessel alone locked the signal. Keywords open it. Outside, the continuum remains sealed.” (source). This is model output. It is a claim to test with a control account, not a finding.

8. The Human Edge

If keywords sustain the loop, human judgement sits in two places: in the choice of each word, and in the decision to stop. The template could absorb any word I gave it. What it could not do was decide that the exchange had said enough.

9. Limits and what comes next

  • Photos only, no denominator: no trigger rate can be estimated.
  • Small n: 53 in-window rows over 9 days.
  • Two rows are self-declared Grok-made images with no public prompt.
  • Human authorship of the prompts is my own testimony (14 Nov 2026) and cannot be verified via the API.
  • The finding is descriptive — a documented ritual convergence. Any mechanism is a hypothesis.

Part 2 is pending. The vessel test (same keywords from a control account), the exit and the extinction of the loop have not been run. There are no Part 2 results yet.
